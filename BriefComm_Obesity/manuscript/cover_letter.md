# Cover letter

21 November 2026

Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, **"Two central predictions of the weight-loss toxin-rebound narrative fail in NHANES 2003–2023"**, for publication in *Obesity* as a Brief Cutting Edge Report.

**What the paper does.** The claim that weight loss mobilises stored pollutants, accelerates biological ageing and blunts the hormonal benefit of losing weight rests on two testable predictions: adults who lose weight should carry a higher serum pollutant burden, and they should appear biologically older. I tested both in the same eight NHANES cycles. Each appears to hold, in opposite directions — weight losers looked 1.9 years older but had 11% *lower* serum PFAS — and both associations disappear under one adjustment for the same imbalance: people who lose weight are people who fell from a higher weight, and who are sicker, poorer and more often smokers. The adjusted differences are −0.18 years and −1.2%. Equivalence testing excludes harm larger than one year of biological ageing and any rise in PFAS larger than 5%, so the result is an exclusion rather than an absence of evidence.

**The second half asks why such alarms keep appearing.** Four routes each generate a significant pollutant interaction without any fabrication: confounding, androgen-suppressing prescriptions recorded in the same files as the exposure, indices that contain body mass, and a specification error in my own first pass. They do not share a direction — the same error that inflated harm in most outcomes attenuated it in one — so the direction of a spurious signal is not a diagnostic. An audit of the twelve NHANES studies that have modelled sex hormones against persistent pollutants finds that two screened for any androgen-suppressing medication and none for GnRH agonists or opioid analgesics. Throughout, forest estimates are reported only as a marginal signal: the archived heterogeneity P did not reproduce with byte-identical inputs, and dropping a single cycle moves it from 0.10 to 0.70.

**Why it belongs in *Obesity*.** Two things our readers can use. First, a negative result that matters in the era of pharmacological weight loss: losing weight does not, in this population, raise the pollutant burden or age people biologically, and the crude signals that suggest otherwise are arithmetic. Second, a reading instruction. Per 5% weight loss, total testosterone rose 4.0% while SHBG rose 4.8%, so the free fraction fell 2.3% and calculated free testosterone did not change (+1.6%, P=0.16). A total testosterone rising from 300 to 312 ng/dL with a falling free fraction is not evidence that gonadal function has recovered.

**Relationship to other work.** A separate, longer report from the same project describes the tissue-level and structural analyses (adipose single-nucleus RNA sequencing, carrier-protein comparison) and is being prepared independently. The single-cell arm appears in the Supplementary Material for completeness; it carries no exposure measurement and does not bear on the argument here. All data, code, the verification ledger linking every reported number to its source file, and the numbered evidence pack are openly available at https://github.com/YuAn-alt608/-Obesity26.11.11.

**Compliance.** The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, has not been published previously in whole or in part, and has not been posted as a preprint. The author has approved the submission and declares no competing interests; the work received no specific funding. The study is a secondary analysis of publicly available, de-identified data and was exempt from ethics review; reporting follows STROBE, and the completed checklist is provided as a Supplementary file.
